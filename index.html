I defended my Ph.D. in Information Studies in September 2026 at the University of Maryland, College Park, where I was advised by Professor Amanda Lazar. Prior to my Ph.D., I earned a B.S. in Computer Science from Virginia Tech, where I worked with Professor Sang Won Lee to design interactive systems that facilitate empathy among users.

My research takes a sociotechnical perspective to understand and advance equitable technology design, particularly for people whose experiences, capacities, and perspectives have historically been marginalized. I examine how assumptions about people shape technology design, how people experience and use technologies in everyday life, and how they can meaningfully influence design decisions. I draw on qualitative, ethnographic, and participatory approaches to pursue these questions.

My research has been published at ACM CHI, CSCW, and ASSETS. My collaborative work received a CHI 2025 Best Paper Award and a CHI 2026 Best Paper Honorable Mention, along with recognition for research excellence from the HCIL Maryland Way Award.

Lines of My Research

Across these lines of work, I study the values embedded in technologies, the realities of technology use, and the conditions under which people can shape design.

Critical & Conceptual

Perspectives in Technology Design

I critically examine how assumptions, values, and dominant ways of knowing become embedded in technology design—and whose perspectives are centered or excluded as a result.

  • “Us” Versus “Them” in Technology Research (CHI 2026, Honorable Mention)
  • Looking Inward in Aging HCI (CHI 2027, under review)
  • Dramaturgical Videoconferencing (ASSETS 2024)
Empirical

Technology in Everyday Life

I investigate how people encounter, interpret, adapt, and sometimes resist technologies in everyday life, with close attention to lived experience and social relationships.

  • Technology Routines (CHI 2025)
  • Looking Beyond the Screen (CHI 2026)
  • Tracking and Memory Concerns (CHI 2025, Best Paper)
  • Older Adult-led Makerspace (CSCW 2024)
Methodological & Design

Participation in Technology Design

I develop methods and design practices that enable people to question researchers’ interpretations and meaningfully shape design decisions.

  • Story Rewriting (CHI 2027, under review)
  • Interactive Tutorials by Demonstration (UIST 2020 Adjunct)
  • Augmenting Existing Interfaces (CHI 2027, under review)
  • Older Adults’ Perspectives on AI (MobiCom4AgeTech 2024)
